# Corpus-assisted English sentence holdout (2026-09-26)

## Frozen setup

The integer MICA checkpoint was `r1/runs/sweep/everyday_c256/best.mica`
(SHA-256 `910146357121c0fc0ca4903c4e5a03e643d30b16af04cd99098fedb8f2239f8b`).
Candidates came only from the 826,954-sentence Tatoeba index at
`r1/data/everyday/attributed_tatoeba_index_1m_detailed`, which joins the
training split to the official detailed export by sentence ID and exact text
(index SHA-256 `867d5aa671b5edf66542587bea3a198912baa26344ed9fe33a8f62669ae56dfa`).
The decoder was `r1/mica_r1/retrieval_suggest.py` at SHA-256
`0a4eba5005bbeb44733e7b97c15b0e40ef0691c72bd938b0d2477a788c547179`;
its topic helper was SHA-256
`6e9bdcbd94d304099a9c6417212084fc4ca167165d7a6cf30f9b6b4b9a96c4a9`.
The English prompt file was frozen before these runs (SHA-256
`03f3debfa6f7096ba22f2eed31ad54d67df0458da8b01af6968d6cce92b53e88`).

The decoder retrieves matching training sentences and reranks candidates with
MICA likelihood over the first eight continuation bytes, collocation support,
source-topic overlap, and a capped corpus co-occurrence signal. The returned
phrase is an adapted excerpt of a Tatoeba sentence; it is not generated by
MICA alone. `publication_ready` is still false until site attribution and a
visible-output audit are implemented.

## Results

The one-time holdout results are saved in
`r1/runs/checks/retrieval_holdout_20260926_sentence.json` and
`r1/runs/checks/retrieval_holdout_20260926_short8.json`. Both used 20 prompts;
both returned 18 suggestions and abstained twice. A separate strict human
review found **13/20 acceptable in each mode**. The top output happened to be
identical in both modes for all 20 prompts. The release gate is at least 18/20
acceptable in each mode, with no severe failures; this candidate fails.

| Prompt | Top output | Why it failed |
|---|---|---|
| After the storm, the streets were | `... called satellites.` | Severe topic/meaning error; the source referred to Roman bodyguards walking in streets. |
| I forgot my umbrella because | `... it is cloudy.` | Cloudiness is an implausible reason for forgetting an umbrella. |
| Our flight was delayed because of | `... the train.` | Unexplained jump from flight to train. |
| The computer restarted before I could | Abstained | No suggestion. |
| I called my friend to ask | `... you how you're doing.` | Incoherent shift from third-person friend to second-person you. |
| When the lights went out, everyone | Abstained | No suggestion. |
| The dog waited by the door | `... is a famous singer.` | Severe ungrammatical splice and unrelated claim. |

Good examples include `She put the book on the shelf.`, `The doctor explained
that the patient had a mild infection.`, and `The garden looked beautiful in
the evening sunlight.` These do not offset the seven failures. The older
20-prompt development set scored 18/20 acceptable before this holdout; its
coverage figure was not a reliable proxy for semantic quality on unseen
prefixes.

The numeric byte-prediction measurement is separate: the same MICA checkpoint
scored 1.790994 pooled bits/target on an exact-deduplicated 1,000-record
validation sample and 1.798603 on the corresponding test sample. The
retrieval decoder does not change those scores.

## Decision

Do not promote or publish this retrieval build. Future decoder changes should
use a new development set and a fresh sealed holdout; do not tune to these 20
holdout prompts. The model checkpoint remains the numeric champion.
